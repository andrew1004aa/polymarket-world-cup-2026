\section*{Impact Statement}

This dissertation contributes a reproducible framework for studying unusually large trades in a continuously repriced prediction market. Its principal methodological value is the combination of market-relative, past-only trade classification; strict temporal price alignment; lead--lag and event-time identification checks; alternative functional forms; out-of-sample prediction; and auditable anomaly screening. The design separates individual trade size from observed wallet activity and cumulative capital deployment without making assumptions about trader identity, ownership, or intent.

The empirical findings are deliberately modest. Large directional flow is followed by a small same-direction price association under both expanding-history and rolling seven-day P99 definitions. The absence of pre-trends and the survival of event-by-time fixed effects strengthen the temporal interpretation, but do not make it causal. The disagreement between linear probability and logit models also demonstrates why a mechanism should not be inferred from one convenient specification. This combination of robustness and restraint offers a useful template for researchers working with event-driven or blockchain-settled markets.

Beyond academia, traders may use the results as a warning against treating an unusually large trade as proof of superior information. Platforms and policy analysts may use the screening framework to prioritise unusual episodes for contextual review, while recognising that a threshold, predictive score, or anomaly rank is not a finding of misconduct. The broader impact is therefore methodological: public trading data can support meaningful behavioural and market-integrity research when association, prediction, and adjudication are kept separate and empirical claims remain proportionate to the evidence.

The dissertation and its versioned analysis code document the classification, alignment, sampling, modelling, and evaluation choices needed to reproduce the reported results. This transparency should make subsequent replication across other prediction markets more feasible while exposing the assumptions on which each conclusion depends.
