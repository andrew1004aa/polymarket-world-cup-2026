\subsection{Extended market-integrity results}
\label{sec:rq3-integrity-results}

Table~\ref{tab:rq3-integrity-results} summarises the supplementary market-integrity evidence.
The results distinguish a systematic concentration association, a systematic
reversal test, out-of-sample predictability, and unusual individual cases.

\subsubsection{MI1: concentration and update incidence}

In the fully controlled model, wallet HHI is positively associated with the
absolute five-minute price change ($\beta=0.000408$, standard error $=0.000036$,
$p<0.001$). A 0.1 increase in HHI corresponds to only a 0.0041 percentage-point
increase in absolute probability movement. The sign differs from the minimal
specification, making activity controls substantively important.

The two-part models locate this association almost entirely in update incidence.
HHI increases the probability of any five-minute price update
($\beta=0.040424$, standard error $=0.003687$, $p<0.001$); a 0.1 increase in HHI
corresponds to a 0.404 percentage-point increase, approximately 11\% of the
3.69\% baseline update rate. Conditional on a non-zero update, HHI is associated
with a slightly smaller absolute movement ($\beta=-0.000259$, $p=0.0275$), but
this result is not robust across the largest-wallet and top-three-wallet-share
proxies. MI1 is therefore conditionally supported on the extensive margin, not
as evidence that concentration reliably increases jump magnitude.

\subsubsection{MI2: no systematic concentration--reversal relation}

None of the six concentration-by-directional-pressure coefficients predicts
partial or full reversal at conventional significance levels. The partial-
reversal estimates are 0.0727, 0.0230, and $-0.0246$ at 15, 30, and 60 minutes;
the corresponding full-reversal estimates are 0.0054, 0.0257, and $-0.0413$.
Unadjusted $p$-values range from 0.430 to 0.904 for the first five estimates and
equal 0.509 at 60 minutes; all six Holm-adjusted $p$-values equal one. HHI-only
robustness models are also insignificant. MI2 is not supported: concentrated
directional activity does not systematically predict a subsequent correction.
This null result does not rule out isolated unusual episodes.

\subsubsection{MI3: incremental out-of-sample prediction}

All MI3 features are observed by approximately minute 5 after the initiating
P99 trade, and the target is subsequent full reversal by minute 30. Accordingly,
MI3 predicts the minute-5-to-minute-30 outcome rather than reversal at trade
initiation. F1 thresholds are selected exclusively from inner out-of-fold
predictions within each outer training fold and are frozen before evaluation on
the held-out fold.

The expanded feature set improves discrimination for every model in grouped
cross-validation. Logistic regression rises from PR-AUC 0.157 and F1 0.225 to
0.236 and 0.298. The expanded random forest performs best, with PR-AUC 0.264,
F1 0.336, and ROC-AUC 0.720. Removing concentration variables reduces random-
forest PR-AUC to 0.199 and F1 to 0.300, while removing follower variables reduces
them to 0.245 and 0.323. Removing trade size does not reduce PR-AUC, indicating
that size alone is not a stable independent ranking signal once the other
features are available.

The chronological holdout yields the same ordering. Expanded logistic
regression obtains PR-AUC 0.340, F1 0.373, and ROC-AUC 0.750, compared with
0.188, 0.248, and 0.586 for its baseline. Expanded random forest obtains PR-AUC
0.337, F1 0.423, and ROC-AUC 0.791, compared with 0.130, 0.182, and 0.427. For
the random forest, the match-cluster bootstrap increase is 0.1218 in PR-AUC
(95\% interval 0.0683--0.1946), 0.1290 in F1 (0.0831--0.1769), and 0.2182 in
ROC-AUC (0.1492--0.2906). MI3 is supported as a predictive claim about the
combined feature set, not as a causal or manipulation claim.

\subsubsection{Anomaly screening and signed case audit}

The transparent screen identifies 104 match episodes and 38 outright episodes
with scores of at least four. The within-family Isolation Forest one-per-cent
review queues contain 534 match and 145 outright episodes. Only 20 episodes are
selected by both methods, reflecting their different purposes: the rule score
requires a narrow conjunction, whereas Isolation Forest ranks unusual
multivariate combinations.

The 73 audited case rows reduce to 35 linked or standalone event units after related
minutes and markets are grouped. The signed dispositions are 38 event-
information cases (52.1\%), 18 not anomalous after context (24.7\%), 12 unusual
but unresolved (16.4\%), and 5 insufficient-data cases (6.8\%). The 12 unresolved
rows reduce further to eight candidate units. No audited case establishes
manipulation. The audit was performed and signed by the researcher and is not
presented as independent external validation.

Cases 060 and 061 provide the most relevant systematic two-sided pattern. The
Australia and Bosnia outright events occur 45 seconds apart, contain nearly
equal observed opposite-direction value, have near-zero flow imbalance, and
produce no price movement. This cross-market regularity is consistent with
systematic two-sided trading or position adjustment. The observed sequence
cannot identify a trading role or establish strategic price control.

Taken together, the supplementary market-integrity analyses find that concentration is associated with whether prices
update and that behavioral/concentration features improve reversal prediction,
but it finds no systematic concentration-driven reversal. Contextual review
further shows that most high anomaly scores reflect live information, terminal
outcomes, or boundary-price mechanics. The defensible contribution is therefore
a reproducible market-integrity screening framework rather than a finding of
misconduct.

\subsubsection{Dynamic past-only wallet heterogeneity and transaction sequences}
\label{sec:wallet-heterogeneity-results}

Table~\ref{tab:wallet-heterogeneity-decisions} reports the final HA1--HA4
decisions. The common evaluation period contains 2,395,771 transaction actions,
279 markets, and 93 match events from 15 June through 22 July. For each UTC
day, the primary rolling definition uses only the preceding seven complete
days. Across the 38 evaluation days, 2,100--32,674 wallets satisfy the activity
requirement and 21--327 are classified in the daily top one per cent. The
expanding definition provides a same-period robustness comparison, while the
full-window definition remains descriptive.

HA1 is supported for ordinary flow. Relative to other wallets' ordinary
actions, same-direction continuation is 0.1466 higher under rolling
classification and 0.1513 higher under expanding classification; paired $t$
and Wilcoxon tests remain significant after Holm adjustment ($p<0.001$). Among
large actions, the corresponding wallet contrasts are positive but the paired
$t$ and Wilcoxon conclusions disagree, so no robust large-action wallet effect
is claimed. The stable finding is persistent ordinary execution by wallets
with high prior activity, not identified order splitting, herding, or market
making.

HA2 is supported as a conditional price association. Under rolling
classification, high activity is associated with higher update incidence among
large trades ($0.006397$, $p<0.001$) and ordinary trades ($0.004262$,
$p<0.001$). Directional-price contrasts are likewise positive for large
($0.000071$, $p<0.001$) and ordinary ($0.000052$, $p<0.001$) flow. The expanding
results have the same signs and significance. Trade size also retains a
positive association within both wallet groups.

HA3 is not robustly supported. In the primary rolling design, the additional
large-by-high-activity interaction is not significant for update incidence
($0.002135$, $p=0.0758$) or directional response ($0.000019$, $p=0.163$). The
expanding update interaction is significant at five per cent ($0.002503$,
$p=0.0331$), but its directional interaction is not ($p=0.272$). By contrast,
the future-informed ex-post interactions are large and significant. This
comparison demonstrates why the ex-post apparent synergy is descriptive and
does not govern inference.

HA4 is not supported as a robust manipulation-consistent pattern. In the
primary rolling design, favourable-opposite contrasts do not survive both the
paired $t$ and Wilcoxon criteria after Holm adjustment. Expanding classification
produces an isolated positive ordinary-wallet contrast, but it is not reproduced
by the primary definition. In the 30-minute full-reversal model, the rolling
and expanding large-by-high-activity interactions are both insignificant
($p=0.608$ and $p=0.550$; Holm-adjusted values $1.000$ and $0.979$). The rolling
high-activity contrast among large flow is negative rather than positive and
is not reproduced by expanding history. The combined evidence supports
persistent execution and separate size/activity price associations, but not a
stable low-buy/high-sell or push--reverse signature.

\subsubsection{Past-only whale-wallet results}
\label{sec:whale-wallet-results}

Table~\ref{tab:whale-wallet-decisions} reports the final WH1--WH4 decisions.
The whale analysis uses the same 2,395,771-action evaluation sample but forms
daily wallet status from all 6,725,732 canonical trades in the 360-market
universe. Under the primary expanding top-one-per-cent definition, the eligible
population grows from 111,419 to 335,414 wallets and the daily whale group from
1,115 to 3,355. No frequency or market-breadth condition is applied.

WH1 is supported as a conditional association. In the primary price-update
model, the whale-minus-other contrast is 0.008470 among large trades and
0.005266 among ordinary trades (both $p<0.001$). Directional-price contrasts
are 0.000058 ($p<0.001$) and 0.000050 ($p<0.001$). Large-trade effects remain
positive within both wallet groups, so whale status and individual execution
size capture distinct dimensions of observed activity.

WH2 is supported for transaction activity and ordinary-flow continuation.
Whales are 0.2155 more likely than other wallets to have another action within
five minutes after a large action and 0.5782 more likely after an ordinary
action. Conditional same-direction continuation is 0.1529 higher for ordinary
whale actions, with both Holm-adjusted paired tests below 0.001. The large-action
continuation contrast is not robust across the paired $t$ and Wilcoxon tests.

WH3 is not robustly supported. The primary expanding update interaction is
positive ($0.003203$, $p=0.0137$), but the directional interaction is not
($p=0.601$). Rolling top one per cent yields $p=0.153$ and $p=0.700$, while the
expanding top-two-per-cent interactions are also insignificant. The stronger
full-window ex-post interactions therefore do not establish a stable synergy.

WH4 receives mixed evidence. After a large action, whales are 0.0466 more
likely to make an opposite action within 60 minutes (both Holm-adjusted tests
$p<0.001$). Conditional on an opposite action, the favourable-price proportion
is 0.1038 higher (adjusted paired $t$ $p<0.001$; adjusted Wilcoxon $p<0.001$),
and this sign is reproduced by rolling top one per cent and expanding top-0.5
and top-two-per-cent definitions. However, the primary 30-minute full-reversal
interaction is insignificant ($p=0.583$, Holm-adjusted $p=1.000$), as are the
rolling and percentile robustness interactions. The sequence evidence is
consistent with active position adjustment or favourable execution after large
actions, but it does not show that whales systematically push prices and then
profit from a market-level correction.

\subsubsection{Continuous cumulative-volume robustness}
\label{sec:continuous-wallet-volume-results}

A threshold-free specification replaces whale status with the within-day
standardised log of each wallet's cumulative gross USDC observed strictly
before that day, re-estimating the same WH1--WH4 sequence and price outcomes.
The results reinforce the activity and favourable-execution components of WH2
and WH4 but do not support a uniformly positive large-by-wallet-scale price
synergy, so WH3 remains unsupported; full coefficients are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:regression-appendix}.
