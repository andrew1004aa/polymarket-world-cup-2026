\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Data sources and canonical trade sample}

This dissertation draws on three linked public sources. Trade-level data were
collected from the Polymarket Data API for the predefined study period.
Market-level trade counts were cross-checked against independently extracted
Dune records. The reconciled Polymarket API dataset, containing 6,725,732 trade
observations across 360 markets, is the canonical research sample. Price-history records come from the CLOB price-history
endpoint at one-minute fidelity; prices are never interpolated or resampled.
Match timing and outcomes come from a separately reviewed FIFA fixture table.

The observation window spans 2026-06-01T00:00:00Z to
2026-08-01T00:00:00Z, with the end point excluded. Within this window the canonical trade sample
contains 6,725,732 records across 360 binary markets: 312 home-win,
away-win, or draw contracts nested in 104 FIFA World Cup matches, and 48
country outright-winner contracts. The associated price dataset contains
17,980,605 raw CLOB price-history points for the 720 corresponding YES and
NO tokens. The data-collection report and data dictionary maintained alongside
this dissertation remain the authority for full source coverage and field-level
definitions and are not reproduced here.

\subsection{Sample separation principles}

Three separations are applied consistently to the canonical sample before it
is aggregated into any model-ready panel. First, match contracts and country
outright contracts are analysed as separate families throughout, because
their horizons, resolution timing, and information environments differ; no
result pools the two families. Second, match-market observations are split
by phase: pre-kickoff observations form the primary sample for H1, H2, H3a, H3b and RQ4,
post-kickoff/pre-resolution observations are analysed separately because
match events generate fundamental information, and trades at or after
recorded resolution are retained only in a method-neutral audit panel and
excluded from every model sample. The post-kickoff phase is not labelled
purely in-play because actual final-whistle timestamps are unavailable.
Third, trades within the same market and UTC minute are aggregated into a
single market-minute observation before any price response is calculated,
because the price-history endpoint has one-minute fidelity and several
trades can share the same observed price outcome.

The wallet-heterogeneity and whale samples share the same 2,395,771-action
evaluation window but classify wallets from different histories: the
high-activity proxy ranks wallets using only their own trailing or expanding
activity, whereas the whale proxy ranks wallets by cumulative gross USDC
across all 6,725,732 canonical trades in the full 360-market universe. The
two proxies are conceptually and operationally distinct (Section~\ref{sec:wallet-heterogeneity-design}, Section~\ref{sec:whale-wallet-design})
and are not interchangeable, even though both are evaluated over the same
action sample.
