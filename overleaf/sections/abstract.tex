\section*{Abstract}

This dissertation examines whether unusually large individual trades are
associated with stronger short-horizon probability adjustment than ordinary
trades on Polymarket. The canonical sample contains 6,725,732 Polymarket Data
API trades across 360 markets related to the 2026 FIFA World Cup; market-level
counts were cross-checked against independently extracted Dune records. Large
trades are classified using market-specific past-only P99 thresholds. Prices
are aligned without interpolation, and the analysis combines fixed-effects
regressions, lead--lag tests, grouped machine learning, and anomaly screening.

Under expanding-history and rolling-seven-day definitions, the large-minus-
ordinary five-minute coefficients are 0.000082 and 0.000081. A tenfold increase
in large-trade net flow corresponds to only about 0.0232 percentage points,
indicating a small economic magnitude. Pre-trade placebo coefficients at
$-15$, $-5$, and $-1$ minutes are insignificant; positive associations appear
from $+1$ through $+15$ minutes and survive event-by-time fixed effects. Linear
probability models find a larger update-incidence association for large flow,
but market-fixed-effects logit models do not reject equality, making that
mechanism interpretation model-sensitive. Features observed during the first
five minutes improve out-of-sample prediction of full reversal by minute 30,
but the model does not classify manipulation.

The evidence supports a precise associational conclusion: relatively large net
flow precedes a small same-direction short-horizon adjustment. It does not
identify trader intent, private information, causal price impact, or misconduct.
