\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This dissertation examined the relationship between unusually large trades and short-horizon probability adjustment on Polymarket during the 2026 FIFA World Cup. Trade-level data were collected from the Polymarket Data API for the predefined study period. Market-level trade counts were cross-checked against independently extracted Dune records. The reconciled Polymarket API dataset, containing 6,725,732 trade observations across 360 markets, was used as the canonical research sample.

The primary design classifies large trades using market-specific P99 thresholds formed only from prior information. Under the expanding-history definition, a market must have at least 1,000 earlier trades through the previous complete UTC day; the rolling specification applies the same requirement to the previous seven complete days. Prices are aligned without interpolation: the baseline is the latest observation strictly before the trade interval, flow is measured within the interval, and the outcome uses the first observed price at or after the target horizon. The analysis combines fixed-effects regressions, lead--lag tests with Holm-adjusted inference, event-by-time fixed effects, alternative binary-response models, supervised prediction, and unsupervised anomaly screening.

The principal finding is a robust but economically small directional association. The large-minus-ordinary five-minute contrasts are 0.000082 under expanding history and 0.000081 under rolling history. In the final strict-timing model, a tenfold increase in large-trade net flow is associated with approximately 0.0232 percentage points of probability movement. Estimates at minus fifteen, minus five, and minus one minute are approximately zero, while positive associations appear at plus one, plus five, and plus fifteen minutes. The plus-thirty-minute result is not robust across both definitions. The association also survives event-by-minute and event-by-five-minute fixed effects, indicating that it is not explained solely by shocks common to contracts within the same match and time cell.

The mechanism evidence is more qualified. Linear probability models suggest that large flow is more strongly associated with whether any price update occurs, but market-fixed-effects logit models find no statistically distinguishable large-versus-ordinary difference. The extensive-margin interpretation is therefore functional-form sensitive and is not treated as the principal result. Likewise, average continuation without Brier-score improvement does not demonstrate superior information. The supervised machine-learning models predict objectively defined thirty-minute full reversal rather than manipulation; thresholds are selected exclusively inside training data and evaluated using event-separated and chronological holdouts. Unsupervised models identify unusual multivariate observations for contextual review, not confirmed misconduct.

These results support a deliberately narrow conclusion: unusually large directional flow is followed by a small short-horizon movement in the same direction, and this temporal pattern is robust to two past-only threshold definitions and demanding event-time controls. They do not establish that the trade caused the movement, that the trader possessed private information, or that anomalous behaviour constitutes manipulation. Wallet-level activity, capital deployment, and individual trade size remain distinct constructs and are interpreted separately.

The dissertation makes three connected contributions. First, it replaces a
future-informed full-window size classification with expanding and rolling
thresholds that can be reproduced using only information available before each
classification day. Second, it combines strict price alignment with placebo
leads, post-trade lags, and event-by-time comparisons, making the temporal basis
of the primary association explicit. Third, it separates explanatory
regression, supervised prediction, and unsupervised anomaly discovery. This
separation prevents predictive performance or unusualness from being presented
as proof of a behavioural mechanism or integrity violation.

The supplementary evidence reinforces the value of this separation. Wallet
activity and prior cumulative capital are associated with several subsequent
transaction patterns, but they are not interchangeable with an unusually large
individual trade. Behavioural and concentration features improve reversal
prediction under event-grouped validation and a chronological holdout, while
fixed-effects reversal tests do not show a general push-and-reverse pattern.
The contextual audit further reduces 73 reviewed rows to 35 linked or standalone
units and establishes no instance of manipulation. These results provide
directions for investigation rather than labels for traders or transactions.

The principal limitations are observational identification, the absence of independently timestamped public-news covariates, imperfect correspondence between wallet addresses and economic owners, dependence on an operational P99 threshold, and external validity from a single high-attention tournament. Even event-by-time fixed effects cannot absorb information specific to one contract within a match-time cell. Likewise, a public address may combine several strategies or represent only part of one economic actor's activity. These measurement limits make causal or identity-based claims inappropriate.

Future work can address these limits by adding independently timestamped match
events, team announcements, injury news, and external betting probabilities;
replicating the past-only design across other sports, elections, and financial
event markets; and preregistering alternative relative-size thresholds. Longer
panels would also permit more demanding tests of whether wallet-level patterns
persist across unrelated events rather than within one tournament. Within the
present boundaries, the dissertation provides a reproducible framework that
keeps empirical claims proportionate to the evidence and shows why economically
small associations can remain statistically informative without being treated
as causal or strategically decisive.
