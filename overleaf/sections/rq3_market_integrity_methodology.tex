\subsection{Extended market-integrity methodology}
\label{sec:rq3-integrity-methodology}

The preceding regressions estimate average flow--price associations, but an
integrity assessment also requires separating concentrated activity, temporary
price pressure, predictability, and unusual individual episodes. The supplementary
market-integrity analysis therefore uses four complementary designs, labelled
MI1--MI3 plus contextual screening to distinguish them from the main H1, H2, H3a, H3b and RQ4.
The first two retain fixed-effects inference;
the third evaluates out-of-sample prediction; and the fourth ranks observations
for contextual review. None of these designs has a verified manipulation label,
so anomaly scores and case flags are not interpreted as proof of misconduct.

\subsubsection{MI1: concentration and price adjustment}

For each market-minute, observed-wallet gross-USDC shares are used to construct
the Herfindahl--Hirschman index (HHI), the largest-wallet share, and the combined
share of the three largest wallets. MI1 tests whether greater within-minute
concentration is associated with the absolute five-minute change in the
YES-equivalent probability. The primary model includes market and unique UTC
calendar-hour fixed effects, with standard errors clustered by FIFA match
event. Controls include the baseline YES price, absolute lagged 30-minute price
change, lagged volume, minutes to kickoff, current gross volume, trade count,
and absolute net-flow imbalance.

Because 96.31\% of five-minute price changes equal zero, a two-part
specification distinguishes the extensive margin from the intensive margin.
The first part is a linear-probability model for whether any price update occurs;
the second conditions on a non-zero update and models its absolute magnitude.
The conditional second part is outcome-selected and is interpreted as a
descriptive robustness check rather than a causal decomposition.

\subsubsection{MI2: concentrated pressure and reversal}

MI2 restricts attention to 19,256 pre-kickoff match-market minutes in which net
flow has a non-zero direction and the five-minute price movement is positive in
that direction. The focal regressor is wallet HHI interacted with absolute
net-flow imbalance. Partial reversal occurs when the later cumulative
directional movement is smaller than the initial five-minute movement; full
reversal occurs when the later directional movement is negative. Separate
linear-probability models are estimated at 15, 30, and 60 minutes with market
and calendar-hour fixed effects and event-clustered covariance. Six primary
interaction tests are adjusted jointly using Holm's method.

\subsubsection{MI3: out-of-sample reversal prediction}

MI3 asks whether behavioural and concentration information observed during the
first five minutes after a P99 trade adds out-of-sample information about full
directional reversal between approximately minute 5 and minute 30. It is not a
prediction made at the instant of the initiating trade. The
pre-kickoff match sample contains 5,843 events, including 748 reversals, grouped
within 104 matches. A baseline feature set (baseline probability and the initial five-minute
directional move) is compared with an expanded set that adds the initiating
trade's size, within-minute wallet concentration (largest- and top-three-wallet
share and HHI), absolute flow imbalance, and follower-wallet flow over the
following five minutes. Logistic
regression, elastic net, histogram gradient boosting, and random forest are
evaluated using nested cross-validation with match-event groups held out, which
limits model-selection bias and respects the grouped dependence structure
\citep{varmasimon2006nestedcv,roberts2017blockedcv}. PR-AUC is the primary metric
because reversals comprise 12.8\% of the sample and precision--recall summaries
are especially informative under class imbalance \citep{saito2015precisionrecall};
F1, ROC-AUC, and Brier score are also reported \citep{ferro2007brier}. Within
each outer training fold, hyperparameters and the F1 decision threshold are
selected from grouped inner out-of-fold predictions. The fitted model and frozen
threshold are then applied to the held-out outer fold, whose labels are used
only for final evaluation. A chronological holdout trains
on the earliest 78 matches and tests on the latest 26, ordered by kickoff, and
match-cluster bootstrap intervals quantify the incremental performance of the
expanded feature set. This supplementary time-ordered check addresses temporal
transportability rather than replacing grouped validation \citep{bergmeir2018timeseriescv}.
Future price outcomes are excluded from model inputs. More broadly, the exercise
is framed as outcome prediction, not automated adjudication of illegal trading
\citep{james2023illegaltrading}.

\subsubsection{Unsupervised screening and contextual audit}

The integrity screen covers all 67,845 P99 trade episodes in the 360 markets.
A transparent rule score records extreme relative size, initiating-minute HHI
of at least 0.90, absolute flow imbalance of at least 0.90, aligned follower
flow, and a substantial observed opposite-direction trade by the initiating
wallet. In parallel, separate Isolation Forest models are fitted to match and
country-outright markets using 500 trees, at most 4,096 observations per tree,
and random seed 20260814 \citep{liu2008isolationforest}. Model inputs contain trading and observed-wallet
sequence features but exclude subsequent price changes and reversal outcomes.
The highest-scoring one per cent is a review-budget flag, not a predicted class.

Multiple P99 trades in the same market-minute are collapsed for qualitative
review. A pre-specified audit sample is drawn from the 50 highest-ranked clusters
in each market family and includes method-agreement clusters, the highest 25
Isolation Forest clusters per family, and transparent-rule additions. The 73
resulting cases are assigned one of five neutral dispositions:
\emph{event information}, \emph{not anomalous after context}, \emph{unusual but
unresolved}, \emph{insufficient data}, or \emph{data artifact}. The audit was
signed by the researcher on 15 August 2026. The label \emph{manipulation} is not
available because there is no independently verified misconduct label and
public addresses do not reveal common ownership or intent.

The supervised and unsupervised components answer different questions.
Supervised models predict the objectively labelled outcome of subsequent price
reversal; they do not classify manipulation. Isolation Forest and transparent
rules instead identify multivariate trading episodes that are unusual without
requiring a verified misconduct label. Contextual case review then assesses
whether those episodes are explained by match information, boundary-price
mechanics, or ordinary execution. Only residual cases are described neutrally
as unusual and potentially manipulation-consistent; neither anomaly rank nor
case retention establishes misconduct.
